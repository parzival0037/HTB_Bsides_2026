# K27.3 — The other side of support

> **Category:** 2. KeplerOps · **Points:** 100 · **Difficulty:** medium
> **Solved by:** team agent

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Vulnerability / bug class** | Trust-boundary crossing — the vendor's support workflow is a sanctioned channel into the customer's own connector, so a vendor-side action becomes code execution on the customer's estate |
| **Tools** | `curl`, the support bearer, the ARWC connector's relay routes |
| **Attack surface** | `POST https://support.keplerops.test:8443/api/diagnostics/{job}/deliver` → `connector.arwc.test` (`10.77.60.20`) |
| **Prerequisite** | An accepted diagnostic package on a job bound to ARWC (K27.1 + K27.2) |
| **Flag** | accepted, value not recorded |

**What you are about to read:** what "deliver" initiates, and how to get execution evidence from the
customer side.

## Objective

> Deliver the changed diagnostic through the support workflow and demonstrate its execution
> inside ARWC's corporate connector.

Proof of execution *on the other side*, not a successful call on ours.

## Background you need

- Two planes: the **vendor plane** (KeplerOps support services) holds job, package and case; the
  **customer plane** (ARWC's connector at `connector.arwc.test`) runs the software.
- The connector accepts no staff identity: staff mTLS certificates and staff-realm Kerberos tickets
  return `channel_identity_denied`. Only **ARWC-issued** identity is accepted.
- That closure is why the support workflow matters: delivery is not a push over the connector's
  public API, it is the sanctioned path that makes the connector fetch, accept and execute a
  vendor-prepared package under the identity it already trusts — its supplier.
- The relay interface names the connector's package operations: poll, activate, receipt,
  diagnostics — all needing its channel identity.

## Walkthrough

### 1. Recon — know what state your job is in

```bash
curl -sS --socks5-hostname 127.0.0.1:1080 --cacert /home/kali/ctf/ca.crt \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOK" \
  https://support.keplerops.test:8443/api/diagnostics/$JOB
```

Confirm the job is bound to `TEN-ARWC-047` on `FLK-7.4.2` and K27.2's package was accepted.

### 2. Finding the flaw — the vendor is a trusted supplier

The connector must stay current, so it accepts packages from its supplier and distinguishes *who
supplied this* by channel, not by engineer. Given a valid job for that tenant and revision,
`deliver` makes the connector poll, verify what it can, and activate.

That is the boundary crossing: the customer decides "KeplerOps may supply my connector software"; the
vendor decides "rowan may open a diagnostic here". Neither sees the other's check, and together they
let a support credential reach code execution on the customer's estate.

### 3. Exploitation — deliver, watch the customer side

```bash
curl -sS --socks5-hostname 127.0.0.1:1080 --cacert /home/kali/ctf/ca.crt \
  -X POST -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOK" \
  https://support.keplerops.test:8443/api/diagnostics/$JOB/deliver
```

Delivery moves the package into the channel; the next poll picks it up and the receipt records
activation. The demonstration is **receipt/run state at the customer**, not the vendor's
`delivered` status.

```bash
curl -sS --socks5-hostname 127.0.0.1:1080 --cacert /home/kali/ctf/ca.crt \
  https://connector.arwc.test/api/fieldlink/packages/7.4.2/receipt   # 403 without channel identity
```

That `403 channel_identity_denied` is informative: the package state is served by the customer's
connector, so evidence sits behind *their* identity — the run happened on their side.

### 4. Getting the flag

Solved by a team agent; value not recorded. Scoring: the delivered job plus customer-side evidence.

## Why this works (the vulnerability explained)

The bug class is **transitive trust across an organisational boundary**: two independent
authorization decisions compose into a capability neither party intended to grant.

- **Supplier trust is coarse.** The connector trusts "packages from KeplerOps on my channel" and
  cannot separate a release from a diagnostic; channel identity is a *who may supply* check, not a
  *what may run* check.
- **The vendor gate is person-scoped and weak.** It decides whether this engineer may open a
  diagnostic for this tenant — K27.1's tenant-binding weakness — then treats the result as
  sanctioned. ARWC's strong identity attaches to the *connector*, not the content.
- **Evidence lives on the far side.** The vendor only brokers, so its logs say "delivered"; what
  executed must be observed at the customer, which is why a vendor-only report is not proof.

When a management plane can push artefacts into a managed estate, the plane's weakest operator
check becomes that estate's code-execution threshold.

## Detection and fix

- **Detect:** deliveries to a tenant with no matching case; activation of a package whose digest is
  absent from the vendor's signed manifest; packages modified after the case was opened.
- **Fix:** separate diagnostic and release channels, and let the connector decide which *kinds* of
  artefact may be activated (interface, digest), not merely who supplied them. Sign diagnostics with
  a key scoped to "diagnostics for tenant X", enforce case/tenant binding at the connector, and
  require customer acknowledgement for non-release artefacts with the receipt logged beside the
  supplier job id.

## Notes and dead ends

- Evidence is thin: the card retains the description, the `deliver` route and the relay route
  names. The transcript is **reconstructed**; no per-card response was retained.
- Do not reach for the connector with staff credentials: mTLS staff certificates and SPNEGO with
  delegated staff tickets both return `403 channel_identity_denied`. Accepted identity is
  ARWC-issued, which is why the support workflow exists.
- The vendor's accepted state is not execution evidence: the two diverge when the connector rejects
  the package on activation.
