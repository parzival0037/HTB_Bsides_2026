**What you are about to read:** how a document *preview* service becomes a confidentiality defect —
the renderer is optimised for fidelity, not authorisation — and how the association record tells you
which identity workflow consumes the preview. The route-level technique is **reconstructed**.

## Objective

> Recover the restricted support-note draft and establish which identity workflow consumes its
> preview.

Two deliverables, and the second is the interesting one. The **draft** is an unpublished, restricted
support note. The **consumer** is the identity workflow that takes a preview of it — something that
binds a person or service identity to a document. Knowing the consumer turns a leak into an
escalation path.

## Background you need

Document pipelines separate `associate` (id + ACL) from `review`, `render`/`prepare` (bytes to
pixels) and delivery — each often a different service, because rendering is heavy.

The classic mistake is that the **association** carries the classification while the **renderer**
trusts the association. Then anyone who can address a document id can render it, and the renderer
returns fidelity content (extracted text, thumbnails, embedded links) the authorising surface would
have redacted.

A second mistake: association records are treated as plumbing and returned in list views — yet they
name the source, the viewer request, the document identity and the consumer that requested the
preview.

## Walkthrough

> reconstruction: real hosts and documented route families, illustrative detail.

### 1. Enumerate the preview surface

```bash
curl -sS https://preview.keplerops.test/associations | jq
```

An association links a *source* (support ticket, attachment, handover) to a *document identity* and
a *viewer request*. If the list route answers without a session, you already have more than the
document's owner intended.

### 2. Follow the association to the draft

Take the association whose source is the ARWC support material and ask for its preview:

```bash
curl -sS "https://preview.keplerops.test/render?document=DOC-…" -o /tmp/preview.bin
```

For a note, "rendered" means extracted text — anything the author marked restricted and unpublished
is still in the extraction, because the renderer only knows about fonts and layout.

### 3. Read the consumer field

The association/review record names the workflow that requested or consumes the preview: an identity
workflow is consuming previews of a restricted support note — so the note is reachable from a path
that also touches identity data, under that workflow's authority rather than the owner's.

### 4. Getting the flag

Accepted; the card record stores no flag, so none is reproduced. The finding is the recovered draft
plus the consumer identification — recovering bytes is easy, attributing them is the work.

## Why this works (the vulnerability explained)

Preview pipelines are a **confused deputy**: the renderer runs with its own service identity and is
asked to materialise a document for a caller whose rights it does not model. Three contributing
errors:

1. **Classification on the wrong object.** The restricted marker sits on the note or association,
   while the render step authorises on "can you address this document id".
2. **Fidelity beats redaction.** Rendering extracts embedded text, attachments and metadata; even a
   "redacted" view leaks through what the renderer considers part of the page.
3. **Previews are cached and shared.** A prepared preview is served by id, so the second reader never
   re-triggers the first reader's authorisation check.

The correct model: the render service receives a signed, short-lived capability encoding caller,
document and permitted fidelity, and refuses to resolve anything outside it. The association list is
scoped to the caller, and the consumer identity is explicit in the request — so "which workflow
consumed this preview" is an audit fact rather than an investigation.

## Detection and fix

- **Log signal:** render/prepare calls where the requesting workflow differs from the document
  owner's tenant or team; a restricted note's id appearing in an association list; preparations for
  notes that were never published.
- **Fix:** pass a capability token (caller + document + fidelity + TTL) into the renderer; make
  `published:false` a hard deny at render; scope association listing; raster restricted previews
  with no text layer. Record `consumer_workflow` per request so the second half of the card is
  answerable from logs alone.
